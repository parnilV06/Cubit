In the previous lesson, you learned that single letters like `R` and `U` turn a face 90 degrees clockwise.

However, speedcubing algorithms rarely use clockwise turns alone. To reverse a move or make larger adjustments, notation uses two essential modifiers: **Prime (`'`)** and **Double (`2`)** turns.

> [!NOTE]
> The apostrophe (`'`) is pronounced **"prime"**. For example, `R'` is spoken aloud as **"R-Prime"** and `U'` as **"U-Prime"**.

---

## 1. Prime Turns (Counter-Clockwise)

An apostrophe after a face letter indicates a **90-degree counter-clockwise turn**, viewed directly from that face.

Because you look at the cube from the front, prime turns move in the opposite visual direction of standard turns:

| Move | Pronunciation | Direction (from front viewpoint) | Inverse of |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`R'`** | *R-Prime* | Pulls the right layer **downwards** (towards you) | `R` |
| **`L'`** | *L-Prime* | Pushes the left layer **upwards** (away from you) | `L` |
| **`U'`** | *U-Prime* | Rotates the top layer **to the right** | `U` |
| **`D'`** | *D-Prime* | Rotates the bottom layer **to the left** | `D` |
| **`F'`** | *F-Prime* | Rotates the front layer **counter-clockwise** | `F` |
| **`B'`** | *B-Prime* | Rotates the back layer **counter-clockwise** | `B` |

### The Inverse Rule:
A standard move and its prime counterpart are exact opposites. Performing a move followed immediately by its prime returns the cube to its original state:

```
R + R' = Solved (No change)
U + U' = Solved (No change)
```

## 2. Double Turns (180 Degrees)

A number **`2`** after a face letter represents a **180-degree double turn** (two quarter turns of the same face).

- **`R2`**: Turn the Right face 180 degrees.
- **`U2`**: Turn the Top face 180 degrees.
- **`F2`**: Turn the Front face 180 degrees.
- **`L2`**: Turn the Left face 180 degrees.
- **`D2`**: Turn the Down face 180 degrees.
- **`B2`**: Turn the Back face 180 degrees.

> [!TIP]
> **Direction Independence**: Because turning a face 180 degrees clockwise or 180 degrees counter-clockwise places the pieces in the exact same physical position, `R2` and `R2'` are identical. You will almost always see it written simply as `R2`.

### Double Turn Cancellation:
Executing any double turn twice completes a full 360-degree rotation, returning that face to its starting state:

```
R2 + R2 = Solved
U2 + U2 = Solved
```

## 3. Combining Modifiers in Algorithms

Real speedcubing algorithms combine standard turns, prime turns, and double turns in rapid sequence.

One of the most famous algorithms in speedcubing is the **Sune** (pronounced *soon-eh*). It is used to orient the top yellow corners and contains standard, prime, and double turns:

```notation
R U R' U R U2 R'
```

## 4. Common Beginner Mistakes

- **Confusing Opposing Prime Directions**: Remember that `R'` moves downwards, but `L'` moves upwards. Always visualize looking directly at the face in question.
- **Over-Rotating Double Turns**: A double turn is exactly 180 degrees (two clicks). Stopping at 90 degrees or turning 270 degrees will scramble your progress.
- **Modifier Scope**: The modifier applies only to the letter immediately before it. In `R U2`, only `U` is turned 180 degrees; `R` is turned 90 degrees clockwise.

---

## 5. Summary & Key Takeaways

- **Standard letter (`R`)**: 90° clockwise turn.
- **Prime (`R'`)**: 90° counter-clockwise turn (inverse of standard).
- **Double (`R2`)**: 180° turn (direction-independent).
- **Inverses cancel**: `R R'` returns to the previous state; `R2 R2` returns to the previous state.
- **Full rotation**: 4 quarter turns (`R R R R`) or 2 double turns (`R2 R2`) equal a full 360° rotation.
