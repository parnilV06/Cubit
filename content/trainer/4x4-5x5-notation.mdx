As cubes get larger, so does the notation needed to control them.

On a 4x4 or 5x5 cube, you have internal layers that do not exist on a 3x3. To write algorithms and scrambles for big cubes, speedcubers use **layer-depth prefixes** and **wide notation**.

> [!NOTE]
> All official World Cube Association (WCA) scrambles for 4x4 and 5x5 use this standardized notation system.

---

## 1. Outer Layers vs. Wide Turns on Big Cubes

On any NxN puzzle, standard single-letter moves operate on only the **single outermost layer**:

- **`R`**: Turns only the 1 outermost right layer.
- **`U`**: Turns only the 1 outermost top layer.
- **`F`**: Turns only the 1 outermost front layer.

When you add a **`w`** suffix, it becomes a wide move turning multiple layers together:

- **On a 4x4**: `Rw` turns the outer **2 layers** on the right side.
- **On a 5x5**: `Rw` also turns the outer **2 layers** on the right side.

```
4x4 SINGLE MOVE (R):    Turns 1 of 4 layers (outer slice only)
4x4 WIDE MOVE (Rw):     Turns 2 of 4 layers (outer half of cube)
```

## 2. Layer-Depth Prefixes on 5x5 Cubes

On a 5x5 cube, there are 5 total layers across each axis. A standard wide turn (`Rw`) turns 2 layers, but what if you need to turn **3 layers**?

To specify turning more than 2 layers, notation places a **number prefix** before the move letter:

| Notation | Cube Size | Layers Turned | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`R`** | 4x4 / 5x5 | 1 Layer | Outermost right layer only |
| **`Rw`** | 4x4 / 5x5 | 2 Layers | Outer 2 right layers together |
| **`3Rw`** | 5x5 | 3 Layers | Outer 3 right layers together (past the center slice) |
| **`3Uw`** | 5x5 | 3 Layers | Outer 3 top layers together |
| **`3Fw2`** | 5x5 | 3 Layers (180°) | Outer 3 front layers turned 180 degrees |

> [!TIP]
> The number prefix defines the **depth** of layers turned from that face. `3Rw` means: *"Turn 3 layers deep, starting from the Right face"*.

## 4. Reading Big-Cube Scrambles

When you compete or practice 4x4 and 5x5 on Cubit, your scramble will look like this:

```notation
Rw2 B2 U2 Fw2 D' L2 B2 D Rw2 B' D' R' F' Rw' B U' Fw'
```

Notice how standard face turns (`B2`, `D'`, `L2`) are mixed with wide turns (`Rw2`, `Fw2`, `Rw'`).

By recognizing that `w` means turning 2 layers deep, you can follow official big-cube scrambles with complete confidence.

---

## 5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Confusing `3Rw` with 3 Consecutive Turns**: `3Rw` does *not* mean turning `Rw` three times. It means turning **3 layers deep** in a single motion.
- **Orientation on Even Cubes**: 4x4 cubes do not have fixed center pieces. When reading notation on a 4x4, ensure you maintain the standard White-Top, Green-Front orientation throughout.
- **Mixing Up Notation Eras**: Older solving guides sometimes used lowercase letters like `2R` or `r` for inner slices. Modern WCA regulations use `Rw` and `3Rw` to ensure universal consistency.

---

## 6. Summary & Key Takeaways

- **Single letters (`R`, `U`)**: Turn 1 outer layer on any size puzzle.
- **Wide moves (`Rw`, `Uw`)**: Turn 2 outer layers simultaneously.
- **Depth prefixes (`3Rw`)**: Turn 3 layers deep from the named face on 5x5+ puzzles.
- **Universal across NxN**: Once you understand face letters, modifiers, and wide depth prefixes, you can read notation for any NxN cube in existence.
