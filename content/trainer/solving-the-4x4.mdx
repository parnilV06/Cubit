The **4x4x4 Cube** (historically named *Rubik's Revenge*) introduces two major structural leaps from the 3x3: **movable center blocks** and **split edge wings**.

Instead of inventing an entirely new method from scratch, speedcubers use the **Reduction Method**: transform the 4x4 into an equivalent 3x3, then solve it using your existing techniques.

```
[ 4x4 Cube Structure ]
• 8 Corners      → Identical to 3x3 corners
• 24 Edge Wings  → Must be paired into 12 composite edges
• 24 Centers     → Must be built into 6 2x2 center blocks
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A 4x4 has **no fixed central centers**. You are responsible for building centers in the correct relative color scheme: White opposite Yellow, Green opposite Blue, and Red opposite Orange.

---

## 1. The 4-Stage Reduction Roadmap

```
[ Stage 1: Build 6 Centers ]
Group 4 center pieces per face into solid 2x2 center blocks.
                 ↓
[ Stage 2: Pair All 12 Edges ]
Match the 24 edge wings into 12 composite edge pairs.
                 ↓
[ Stage 3: Solve as a 3x3 ]
Treat 2x2 centers as single centers and paired edges as single edges!
                 ↓
[ Stage 4: Fix Parity (If Encountered) ]
Resolve OLL or PLL parity using targeted big-cube algorithms.
```

---

## 2. Stage 1: Building the 6 Centers

To construct the 2x2 center blocks without disturbing already-solved faces:
1. **Solve White Center**: Group two 1x2 bars of white center pieces on one face.
2. **Solve Yellow Center**: On the opposite face, insert yellow bars using the half-turn insert technique: `Rw U2 Rw'`.
3. **Solve the Lateral 4 Centers**: Remember the **BOY** color arrangement around the sides (with White on Top, reading clockwise: **Blue → Orange → Green → Red**).

## 3. Stage 2: Pairing the 12 Edges

Each 4x4 edge consists of two individual **wings** sharing matching colors (for example, White-Red and White-Red).

We pair them using the **Slice-Flip-Slice** technique:
1. Position two matching wings in the front-left and front-right slots across different horizontal layers.
2. **Slice**: Turn `Uw'` to connect the two wings into a pair.
3. **Flip**: Execute the standard edge-flipping trigger `(R U R' F R' F' R)` to preserve the pair and replace it with an unsolved edge.
4. **Restore Slice**: Turn `Uw` to restore all center blocks.

```notation
Uw' R U R' F R' F' R Uw
```

## 4. Stage 3: Solving as a Reduced 3x3

Once all 6 centers are solid 2x2 blocks and all 12 edges are paired, the puzzle is functionally a 3x3!

```
Composite 2x2 Center  →  Acts as 1 Center
Composite 1x2 Edge    →  Acts as 1 Edge
Single Corner Piece   →  Acts as 1 Corner
```

Proceed through your standard 3x3 solving method (Cross, F2L, OLL, and PLL) using only outer-layer turns (`R`, `U`, `F`, `L`, `D`, `B`).

---

## 5. Stage 4: 4x4 Parity Explained

Because an even-layered cube allows independent internal slice permutations, you may encounter two special states that are mathematically impossible on a standard 3x3:

### Case 1: OLL Parity (Single Edge Flip)
During top-layer orientation, you may find that **only 1 or 3 yellow edges are oriented** (a 3x3 always has 0, 2, or 4).

Fix this single flipped composite edge with the standard OLL Parity algorithm:

```notation
Rw2 B2 U2 Lw U2 Rw' U2 Rw U2 F2 Rw2 F2 Lw' B2 Rw2
```

### Case 2: PLL Parity (Opposite Edge Swap)
During the final permutation step, you may find that **only two opposite edges need to swap** (or only two corners need to swap).

Fix this with the 4x4 PLL Parity algorithm:

```notation
Rw2 R2 U2 Rw2 R2 Uw2 Rw2 R2 Uw2
```

## 6. Key Takeaways

- **Reduction Concept**: Reduce centers and edges first, then solve like a 3x3.
- **Color Scheme Responsibility**: You must build centers in the correct order (White ↔ Yellow, Blue → Orange → Green → Red).
- **Edge Pairing**: Use `Uw' (R U R' F R' F' R) Uw` to pair all 12 composite edges.
- **Parity is Normal**: OLL parity flips a single edge; PLL parity swaps two opposite edges. Both have reliable, dedicated algorithms.
