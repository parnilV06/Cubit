When complete beginners turn a Rubik's Cube, they grab entire faces with their palms and twist using their whole wrists or forearms. This method is slow, exhausts your hands within minutes, and caps your turning speed at around 1.5 to 2 turns per second.

In speedcubing, turning is powered by **Fingertricks**: precise, isolated flicks using individual fingers while your hands remain anchored in a stable **home grip**.

> [!NOTE]
> Modern speedcubes feature internal magnetic positioning and curved piece geometry ("corner cutting") designed specifically for lightweight fingertricks. You do not need brute force to turn the cube; a light flick of a single finger joint is sufficient.

---

## 1. The Speedcuber Home Grip

Every fingertrick starts and ends in the standard **Home Grip**. Holding the cube correctly stabilizes the core and keeps your index, middle, and ring fingers free to turn any layer:

```
[ Left Hand ]                                [ Right Hand ]
• Left Thumb: Front-Left Center/Edge         • Right Thumb: Front-Right Center/Edge
• Left Middle & Ring: Back-Left Face        • Right Middle & Ring: Back-Right Face
• Left Index Finger: Hovers over U-Layer    • Right Index Finger: Hovers over U-Layer
• Left Pinky: Resting underneath             • Right Pinky: Resting underneath
```

When holding the cube in the home grip, your wrists remain stationary. The cube does not wobble, and your fingers can reach five of the six faces instantly.

## 2. The Core Fingertrick Repertoire

Mastering these five fundamental fingertricks allows you to execute almost any speedcubing algorithm without losing control:

| Move | Primary Finger | Execution Technique | Key Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`U`** | Right Index Finger | Pull the top-right back corner forward/leftward in a quick flick. | Fast single-finger top turn. |
| **`U'`** | Left Index Finger | Pull the top-left back corner forward/rightward in a quick flick. | Fast counter-clockwise top turn. |
| **`U2`** | Right Index $\to$ Middle | **Double Flick**: Index finger flicks first 90°, immediately followed by Middle finger for the second 90°. | Executes 180° turn in one fluid motion without regripping. |
| **`F`** | Right Index Finger | Push the top-right front corner down along the front face. | Avoids rotating the right wrist forward. |
| **`F'`** | Right Thumb | Push the bottom-right front corner upward using the thumb joint. | Clean reset after an `F` move. |
| **`D` / `D'`** | Ring Fingers | Left/Right ring finger pushes or pulls the bottom layer corners. | Keeps top grip stable during bottom-layer turns. |

## 3. The `U2` Double Flick

The single most important speedcubing fingertrick is the **Index-Middle Double Flick** for 180-degree turns (`U2` and `U2'`):

```
Step 1: Right Index finger pulls top layer 90°
                  ↓ (Instant follow-through)
Step 2: Right Middle finger pulls top layer another 90°
                  ↓
Result: Full 180° turn in under 0.15 seconds without moving wrists!
```

Never turn `U2` by flicking your index finger twice in separate strokes. Practice chaining index into middle as a single continuous twitch.

## 4. Eliminating Regrips

A **regrip** occurs whenever you remove your hand from the cube, rotate your palm, and re-grab the puzzle in a different position.

Each unnecessary regrip costs **0.2 to 0.4 seconds**. In a 60-move solve, making 10 regrips adds up to 4 full seconds of wasted time!

## 5. Light Grip vs. Heavy Grip

Many beginners squeeze the cube tightly with white knuckles. Squeezing creates friction inside the puzzle, strains hand muscles, and causes catastrophic lockups when a turn slightly misaligns.

- **Hold lightly**: Hold the cube with just enough pressure to keep it from slipping.
- **Let the magnets work**: Allow the cube's internal magnets to snap each layer into alignment.
- **Smooth before fast**: Focus on accurate 90-degree stops before attempting maximum velocity.

---

## 6. Common Fingertrick Mistakes

- **Wrist Twisting for `U` turns**: Grabbing the top layer with your whole hand instead of flicking with the index finger.
- **Double Index Flick for `U2`**: Flicking `U` with the index, resetting the index, and flicking `U` again. Always use Index followed by Middle finger.
- **Over-Tight Grip**: Squeezing the puzzle too hard, which locks the mechanism and strains your tendons.
- **Excessive Regripping**: Letting go of the cube before every single trigger. Keep your thumbs anchored to the front face.

---

## 7. Key Takeaways

- **Anchor in Home Grip**: Thumbs in front, middle/ring fingers behind, index fingers hovering on top.
- **Master the `U2` double flick**: Index finger followed immediately by middle finger.
- **Eliminate regrips**: Execute triggers and algorithms without removing your hands from the puzzle.
- **Turn lightly and accurately**: Precision and loose grip prevent lockups and build effortless speed.
