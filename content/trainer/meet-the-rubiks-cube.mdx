The Rubik's Cube is the world's most famous three-dimensional puzzle. Before learning how to solve it, you need to understand what this object actually is and how its pieces behave.

When you look at a scrambled cube, it can seem like complete chaos. But every cube follows strict mechanical rules. Once you understand the structure, the puzzle becomes approachable.

> [!NOTE]
> Standard speedcubing uses the Western color scheme: **White is opposite Yellow**, **Green is opposite Blue**, and **Red is opposite Orange**.

---

## 1. The Six Faces and Colors

A classic 3x3 Rubik's Cube has six flat sides called **faces**. Each face has its own color when solved:

| Face Name | Position | Standard Color | Opposite Face |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Up** | Top | White | Down (Yellow) |
| **Down** | Bottom | Yellow | Up (White) |
| **Front** | Facing You | Green | Back (Blue) |
| **Back** | Facing Away | Blue | Front (Green) |
| **Right** | Right Side | Red | Left (Orange) |
| **Left** | Left Side | Orange | Right (Red) |

## 2. The Three Fundamental Piece Types

A 3x3 cube may look like 54 individual colored stickers, but it is actually made of **26 physical pieces** moving around a central core.

Every piece on the cube belongs to one of three distinct categories:

| Piece Type | Total on Cube | Visible Colors | Placement | Key Mechanical Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Center** | 6 pieces | 1 color | Middle of each face | Mechanically fixed to core; defines face color. |
| **Edge** | 12 pieces | 2 colors | Between two corners | Connects 2 faces; can only occupy edge slots. |
| **Corner** | 8 pieces | 3 colors | Outer 8 vertices | Connects 3 faces; can only occupy corner slots. |

### 1. Center Pieces (6 Total)
- **Colors:** 1 visible color.
- **Location:** The exact middle of each face.
- **Behavior:** Centers are mechanically attached to the internal core. They rotate in place, but they **never change position relative to each other**.
- **Rule:** The color of the center piece tells you what color that entire face must be when solved.

### 2. Edge Pieces (12 Total)
- **Colors:** 2 visible colors.
- **Location:** Between two corners on the edges of each layer.
- **Behavior:** Edges travel around the cube during turns, but an edge piece can **only ever go into an edge slot**. It can never become a corner.

### 3. Corner Pieces (8 Total)
- **Colors:** 3 visible colors.
- **Location:** At the eight outer vertices of the cube.
- **Behavior:** Corners move between corner positions. A corner piece can **only ever go into a corner slot**.

---

## 3. Pieces vs. Stickers

The most common beginner trap is thinking about a Rubik's Cube as 54 independent colored squares.

A cube is not solved by moving individual colors. It is solved by placing **entire physical pieces** into their correct slots:

- When you move a white-green edge piece, both the white sticker and the green sticker move together.
- You cannot separate the red, white, and blue colors of a corner piece. They are permanently molded into a single plastic block.

> [!TIP]
> Always think in **pieces**, not stickers. When searching for where a piece belongs, look at all of its colors, not just one.

---

## 4. Common Beginner Pitfalls

- **Attempting to solve face by face**: Beginners often try to solve one full color (like all white), then try to solve red next. This destroys the white face. Cubes are solved **layer by layer**, placing pieces where all of their colors match their neighboring centers.
- **Forgetting center positions**: If you are solving the white cross, a white-red edge piece must line up with both the white center and the red center.

---

## 5. Key Takeaways

- **26 moving pieces**: 6 centers, 12 edges, 8 corners.
- **Centers are fixed**: The center piece defines the color of that entire face.
- **Pieces have fixed slots**: Corners only fit in corner slots; edges only fit in edge slots.
- **Solve in 3D**: You are moving complete multi-colored pieces into their correct places.
