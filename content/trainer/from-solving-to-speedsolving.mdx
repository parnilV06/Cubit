Solving a Rubik's Cube for the first time is a massive milestone. But once you can reliably solve the puzzle from any scramble, a new challenge begins: **speedcubing**.

Speedsolving is not simply turning your hands faster. Trying to turn frantically with beginner mechanics leads to lockups, hand fatigue, and longer pauses. True speedcubing is a systematic discipline of **planning, move efficiency, fingertrick ergonomics, and unbroken rhythm**.

> [!NOTE]
> Speedcubing turns a logic puzzle into an athletic skill. The fastest solvers in the world solve the cube in under 4 seconds not because their hands move at superhuman speeds, but because they eliminate wasted moves and never pause to search.

---

## 1. The Speedsolving Formula

Every solve time is governed by a straightforward mathematical relationship:

$$\text{Solve Time} = \frac{\text{Total Move Count}}{\text{Turns Per Second (TPS)}} + \text{Total Pause Time}$$

To make your solves faster, you can adjust three variables:

1. **Reduce Move Count**: Solve the same state in fewer turns.
2. **Increase TPS**: Turn the layers quickly and accurately using fingertricks.
3. **Eliminate Pause Time**: Recognize pieces in advance so your hands never stop moving.

Beginners often focus 100% of their energy on increasing TPS (turning faster). However, pausing for 3 seconds while hunting for a piece destroys your time far more than turning at a relaxed, steady speed.

| Solving Style | Move Count | Turning Speed (TPS) | Pause Time | Total Solve Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Untrained Beginner** | 120 moves | 1.5 TPS | 35 seconds | **115 seconds (1:55)** |
| **Fast-Turning Beginner** | 120 moves | 3.5 TPS | 28 seconds | **62 seconds (1:02)** |
| **Efficient Intermediate** | 60 moves | 3.0 TPS | 6 seconds | **26 seconds** |
| **Advanced Speedcuber** | 52 moves | 6.5 TPS | 0.5 seconds | **8.5 seconds** |

Notice that the Efficient Intermediate turns **slower** (3.0 TPS) than the Fast-Turning Beginner (3.5 TPS), yet finishes in less than half the time because of fewer moves and minimal pauses.

## 2. The Five Pillars of Speedcubing

To transition from casual solving to competitive speedsolving, you must develop five core capabilities:

```
[ 1. Pre-Solve Inspection ]
Scan piece locations and plan your first steps before starting the timer.
            ↓
[ 2. Move Efficiency ]
Use direct, optimal solutions instead of repetitive setup cycles.
            ↓
[ 3. Fingertricks & Ergonomics ]
Flick faces with individual fingers without changing your hand grip.
            ↓
[ 4. Lookahead & Piece Tracking ]
Track future pieces with your eyes while muscle memory turns current moves.
            ↓
[ 5. Continuous Solve Flow ]
Eliminate stop-and-go hesitations to maintain uninterrupted turning.
```

## 4. Feeling the Rhythm: The 4-Move Trigger

In speedcubing, individual moves are grouped into smooth, automatic reflexes called **triggers**. The most fundamental trigger is the **Right Trigger** (often called the *Sexy Move*): `R U R' U'`.

## 5. Common Beginner Speedsolving Pitfalls

- **Turning Before Knowing the Solution**: Turning fast while guessing where pieces will land causes lockups, mistakes, and long pauses. Never turn faster than you can think.
- **Whole-Wrist Twisting**: Grabbing the entire cube face with your palm and twisting your wrist wastes energy and destroys stability. Always use finger flicks.
- **Obsessing Over Hardware**: A modern magnetic speedcube helps prevent misalignments, but hardware cannot replace inspection planning and move efficiency. Master the fundamentals first.
- **Ignoring the Bottom Layer**: Beginners solve on top and flip the cube repeatedly. Speedcubers solve the cross directly on the bottom face (`D`) to keep the top two layers visible.

---

## 6. Key Takeaways

- **Time equals Moves divided by TPS plus Pauses**: Reducing move count and eliminating pauses yields the largest time drops.
- **Efficiency beats raw speed**: A smooth 3 TPS solve with 55 moves is faster than a frantic 5 TPS solve with 110 moves.
- **Think before you turn**: Every speedsolve begins with pre-solve inspection.
- **Master triggers with muscle memory**: Automate short sequences so your eyes can look ahead.
