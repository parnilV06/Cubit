In casual solving, you scramble the cube, start a timer, and immediately begin hunting for pieces while the clock runs. This guarantees a slow, hesitant start with 5 to 10 seconds of wasted time on the very first step.

In speedcubing, every solve begins with **Inspection**: a dedicated 15-second pre-solve window where you examine the cube, locate target pieces, and plan your opening moves before starting the timer.

> [!NOTE]
> Under official World Cube Association (WCA) regulations (Article A3), competitors receive up to 15 seconds of inspection. The judge alerts at 8 seconds and 12 seconds. Starting the solve between 15.00s and 17.00s adds a +2 second penalty, while exceeding 17.00s results in a DNF (Did Not Finish). In Cubit's Timer, you can enable WCA-style inspection countdowns to practice under realistic competition conditions.

## 1. The 15-Second Inspection Breakdown

Fifteen seconds is plenty of time to fully solve the cross in your head. To use inspection effectively, divide the time into three structured phases:

```
[ Phase 1: Scan (0 – 4 Seconds) ]
Find the White center and identify the locations and orientations of all 4 white edges.
            ↓
[ Phase 2: Plan (5 – 10 Seconds) ]
Mentally simulate the move sequence to place each edge into its correct slot.
            ↓
[ Phase 3: Grip & Execute (11 – 15 Seconds) ]
Set your hands into the starting home grip. Place the cube down, start timer, and turn immediately.
```

---

## 2. Phase 1: Rapid 3D Scanning (0 to 4 Seconds)

When you pick up the cube, do not look at stickers randomly. Follow a structured scan:

1. **Locate White Center**: Place the White center on the bottom (`D`) face or facing you.
2. **Find the 4 Cross Edges**: Spot the White-Green, White-Red, White-Blue, and White-Orange edge pieces.
3. **Check Edge Orientations**:
   - Is the white sticker facing up, down, or sideways?
   - Are any edges already solved or one turn away from their target slot?

## 3. Phase 2: Planning the Solution (5 to 10 Seconds)

Once you locate the four edges, mentally simulate how moving one edge affects the others.

### Step-by-Step Mental Simulation Example

Suppose inspection reveals the following setup:
- **White-Green edge** is at `U` face, green matching the front center.
- **White-Red edge** is at the right layer, needing an `R2` turn.
- **White-Blue edge** is one turn from alignment at the back.

Instead of turning the physical cube, trace the moves in your mind:
1. `F2` places the White-Green edge on the bottom.
2. `R2` places the White-Red edge on the bottom.
3. `B2` places the White-Blue edge on the bottom.

By stringing these moves together mentally (`F2 R2 B2`), you enter the solve knowing the exact first 3 moves with zero hesitation.

---

## 4. Phase 3: Grip, Start, and Flow (11 to 15 Seconds)

During the final 4 seconds of inspection:

1. **Position Your Hands**: Place your fingers in the exact positions needed to execute the first move.
2. **Do Not Over-Inspect**: Once your cross sequence is planned, stop scanning. Staring too long causes mental overload and second-guessing.
3. **Commit and Execute**: Place your hands on the timer (or keyboard spacebar). The instant the timer starts, execute your planned cross moves at full rhythm without pausing to check each piece.

## 5. Inspection Habit Comparison

| Habit | Beginner Solver | Speedcuber |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timer Start** | Starts timer immediately without looking | Takes 8 to 12 seconds to inspect before touching timer |
| **First Move** | Searches 3 to 5 seconds after timer starts | First move occurs within 0.1 seconds of timer start |
| **Planning Depth** | Looks for 1 piece at a time during solve | Plans 3 to 4 cross edges completely in advance |
| **Visual Focus** | Stares at moving hands | Eyes already looking for the first corner/edge pair |
| **Average Cross Time** | 8 to 15 seconds | 1.5 to 3.0 seconds |

---

## 6. Common Inspection Mistakes

- **Aimless Staring**: Looking at colors without calculating specific move sequences. Always ask: *"What 3 to 6 moves solve these edges?"*
- **Planning Too Far Ahead Too Early**: Do not try to plan the entire First Two Layers (F2L) yet. Master planning 2 edges, then 3 edges, then the full 4-edge cross.
- **Turning the Cube During Inspection**: In official competitions, turning a layer during inspection results in an immediate penalty or DNF. Keep turns purely mental.
- **Dropping the Plan on Timer Start**: Forgetting your planned moves the moment the timer starts. Trust your inspection and execute decisively.

---

## 7. Key Takeaways

- **Use the full 15 seconds**: Never start a timed solve without inspecting.
- **Follow the 3 phases**: Scan piece locations (0–4s), Plan the cross sequence (5–10s), Set grip and execute (11–15s).
- **Eliminate start hesitation**: Your first move should begin the millisecond the timer starts.
- **Progressive planning**: Start by planning 2 cross edges, then build up to planning the complete cross.
