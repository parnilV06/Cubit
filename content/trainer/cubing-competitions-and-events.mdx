Attending your very first speedcubing competition is an exhilarating milestone.

If you are worried that you are "not fast enough" or that tournaments are only for world-record holders, rest assured: **cubing competitions are open to solvers of every skill level**. Whether your average is 10 seconds or 2 minutes, the community is exceptionally welcoming, supportive, and eager to help newcomers.

---

## 1. Before the Competition: Finding & Registering

```
[ Step 1: Browse Official Competitions ]
Visit worldcubeassociation.org/competitions and filter by your country and city.

[ Step 2: Review Events & Cutoffs ]
Check the competition schedule. Ensure you meet any posted "time limits" (e.g., 3x3 time limit is typically 5 to 10 minutes per solve).

[ Step 3: Register Early ]
Many regional competitions reach competitor capacity within minutes of registration opening. Create a WCA account and register promptly.
```

### What to Pack in Your Bag:
- **Your Main Puzzles**: Clean, tensioned, and properly lubricated.
- **Backup Cube**: In case of a broken piece or pop.
- **Water Bottle & Light Snacks**: Tournaments are full-day events.
- **Microfiber Cloth**: For wiping your cube and warming your hands.

## 2. Arriving at the Venue: The First-Time Timeline

Here is what happens from the moment you walk through the venue doors:

```
[ 1. Check-In & Badges ]
Arrive 30 minutes before the opening ceremony. Check in at the registration desk to receive your name badge and personal schedule card.
                  ↓
[ 2. Attend the New Competitor Tutorial ]
Delegates host a 15-minute live demonstration of Stackmat timers, inspection rules, and judging protocols for first-timers.
                  ↓
[ 3. Group Calling ]
Competitors are divided into groups (e.g., "3x3 Round 1 — Group 3"). When your group is called, bring your puzzle to the drop-off table.
                  ↓
[ 4. Solve at Your Station ]
Take your seat at the assigned solving station when your name is called by a runner or judge.
```

---

## 3. The Official Solving Station Ritual: Step-by-Step

The official solve follows a precise, standardized procedure to ensure fairness:

```
[ 1. Puzzle Placed in Cover ]
The judge brings your scrambled cube covered by an opaque box and places it on the timer mat.

[ 2. Ready? ]
The judge asks, "READY?" When you confirm ("Yes" or a nod), the judge lifts the cover.

[ 3. The 15-Second Inspection ]
The judge starts a stopwatch. You have 15 seconds to inspect the cube without making any turns.
• At 8 seconds, the judge calls out: "8 SECONDS"
• At 12 seconds, the judge calls out: "12 SECONDS"

[ 4. Starting the Timer ]
Place your hands flat on the Stackmat timer sensors (fingers and palms touching pads, not the cube).
When the green LED lights up, lift your hands and begin solving!

[ 5. Stopping the Timer ]
When the cube is fully solved, drop the cube and place both palms flat on the timer pads.
Do NOT touch or move the cube after stopping the timer!

[ 6. Result Verification & Sign ]
The judge inspects the puzzle, writes your time on the official score sheet, and signs it.
Check the written time, sign the sheet, and celebrate your official solve!
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Timer Etiquette**: Never stop the Stackmat timer with the cube in your hands, or with your wrists. Place both hands flat onto the rubber sensors and leave the puzzle untouched until the judge verifies the state.

## 4. Understanding Cutoffs vs. Time Limits

On competition schedules, you will notice two terms:
- **Time Limit**: The maximum time allowed for a single solve (e.g., 5:00 for 3x3). If you exceed the time limit, the solve is recorded as DNF.
- **Cutoff (Cumulative or Best of 2)**: Used in longer events (like 4x4 or 5x5) to keep the schedule on track. You must achieve at least one solve under the cutoff time during your first two attempts to unlock the remaining solves of the average.

## 5. Competition Etiquette & Community Norms

Speedcubing competitions thrive on mutual respect and volunteer participation:

| Practice | Official Rule or Etiquette? | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Silence During Blindfolded Events** | Official Rule | Competitors need absolute silence to memorize and solve without sight. |
| **No Flash Photography** | Official Rule | Bright flashes distract active competitors at solving stations. |
| **Do Not Discuss Scrambles** | Official Rule | Revealing scramble details gives an unfair advantage to upcoming groups. |
| **Volunteering as a Judge or Runner** | Community Norm | WCA competitions are 100% volunteer-run. Helping out makes events run smoothly for everyone! |
| **Cheering & Congratulating Others** | Community Norm | Support your fellow cubers when they achieve personal bests (PBs). |

## 6. Summary Checklist for Your First Tournament

- [ ] Check in early and attend the First-Time Competitor Tutorial.
- [ ] Memorize the inspection cues (8 seconds and 12 seconds).
- [ ] Release the cube before placing both hands on the timer pads to stop.
- [ ] Check the written time on your score sheet before signing.
- [ ] Have fun, meet new friends, and celebrate your personal bests!
