In the beginner method, you learned to construct the White Cross using the Daisy strategy: placing four white edges around the yellow center on top, matching their side colors one by one, and turning each face 180 degrees down.

While the Daisy is simple to understand, it is highly inefficient for speedsolving:
- It requires **12 to 18 total moves**.
- It forces you to rotate the whole cube repeatedly.
- It requires a 180-degree flip before you can begin the next step.

In competitive speedcubing, you solve the cross **directly on the bottom face (`D`) in 8 moves or fewer**.

> [!NOTE]
> Mathematical analysis shows that **every possible 3x3 scramble can have its cross solved in 8 moves or fewer**. Over 99.9% of scrambles can be solved in 7 moves or fewer, and the average optimal cross requires only 5.8 moves.

---

## 1. The Four Principles of an Efficient Cross

To reduce your cross move count from 15 moves down to 6 moves, follow four core principles:

### Principle 1: Always Solve on the Bottom Face (`D`)
Hold the White center on the **Bottom (`D`)** from the start of inspection through the entire solve:
- **No Cube Flip**: You avoid wasting 1 to 2 seconds turning the cube upside down.
- **Immediate Visibility**: The top layer (`U`) and middle layer remain clearly visible, allowing you to spot your first corner/edge pair while executing the cross.

### Principle 2: Memorize Relative Color Order
You do not need to look at center pieces to know where an edge belongs. The standard Western color scheme has fixed relationships:

```
          [ Top: Yellow ]
 [ Left: Orange ] [ Front: Green ] [ Right: Red ] [ Back: Blue ]
          [ Bottom: White ]
```

When White is on the bottom:
- **Green** is opposite **Blue**.
- **Red** is opposite **Orange**.
- Looking at **Green** in front, **Red** is on the Right, and **Orange** is on the Left.

### Principle 3: Solve Relativistically, Then Align (`D` Turn)
You do not need each edge to match its side center immediately upon insertion. As long as the edges are placed in the correct **relative order** to each other, a single bottom-layer turn (`D`, `D'`, or `D2`) will align all four edges with their side centers simultaneously!

### Principle 4: Zero Whole-Cube Rotations
Never perform a `y` or `x` whole-cube rotation during the cross. Every edge on the cube can be placed using `U`, `D`, `L`, `R`, `F`, and `B` moves from a single stationary grip.

## 2. Worked Example: Direct Cross in 5 Moves

Consider a scrambled cube where cross edges are scattered across the top and middle layers.

Instead of building a daisy:
1. Turn `D` to place the target slot beneath the first edge.
2. Insert the edge with `R'`.
3. Insert the next adjacent edge with `F`.
4. Restore the bottom layer with `D'`.

## 3. The 3 Basic Edge Insertion Cases

Every cross edge falls into one of three fundamental scenarios:

| Scenario | Location & Orientation | Direct Solution (White on Bottom) | Move Count |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Easy Insert** | White sticker on side face, matches center | Single face turn (`F2`, `R2`, `L2`, `B2`) or quarter turn (`F`, `R`) | 1 move |
| **Middle-Layer Edge** | Edge in middle layer, white facing front/side | Move slot into position with `D`, turn side face down, restore `D'` | 2 to 3 moves |
| **Flipped Edge on Top** | White sticker facing up on `U` face | Align above target side, execute `F' R U' R'` or `U R' F R` | 3 to 4 moves |

## 5. Daily Cross Training Drills

1. **Unlimited Inspection Cross**: Take as long as you need during inspection (1 to 2 minutes if necessary). Find all 4 edges and calculate the exact move sequence in advance. Close your eyes and execute the entire cross blindfolded.
2. **Move-Count Constraint Drill**: Scramble the cube and find a cross solution in 7 moves or fewer. If your solution takes 9 moves, undo and find where you can combine moves or use `D` layer setup turns.
3. **No-Rotation Rule**: Put a piece of tape or marker on your cube's front face. Solve the cross without ever turning the cube on the `y` or `x` axis.

---

## 6. Common Cross Mistakes

- **Building on Top**: Solving the white cross on `U` out of habit. Force yourself to keep White on `D` from move one.
- **Obsessive Center Matching**: Forcing each edge to match its side center before inserting. Insert pieces in correct relative order (Green next to Red, etc.), then align the whole cross with `D` at the end.
- **Using 4 Setup Moves for 1 Edge**: Over-complicating inserts. If an edge takes more than 3 moves to insert, another edge should be placed first to simplify the sequence.

---

## 7. Key Takeaways

- **Target under 8 moves**: Every cross can be solved in 8 moves or fewer; aim for an average of 6 moves.
- **White stays on Bottom (`D`)**: Eliminates cube flips and allows instant transition to the next step.
- **Relative ordering saves moves**: Solve edges relative to one another, then align all four with a single `D` or `D'` turn.
- **Zero rotations**: Keep your grip stationary and use fingertricks.
