Standard notation moves like `R` and `U` turn a single outer layer.

In intermediate and advanced algorithms, you will often turn **two layers simultaneously**. These are called **wide moves**.

> [!NOTE]
> In official WCA notation, wide moves are designated with a lowercase **`w`** suffix (such as **`Rw`**, **`Uw`**, **`Fw`**). In informal community algorithm sheets, you may also see them written as single lowercase letters (such as **`r`**, **`u`**, **`f`**). Cubit supports both formats.

---

## 1. Single Face Turns vs. Wide Turns

A wide move grips the outer face and the adjacent middle slice layer together, turning both layers as a single unit:

- **Single Face Turn (`R`)**: Turns only the 1 outer right layer.
- **Wide Move (`Rw`)**: Turns the outer right layer **plus the middle slice layer** together (2 layers deep).

```
SINGLE TURN (R):        Turns 1 outer layer
WIDE TURN (Rw):         Turns 2 outer layers simultaneously
```

| Wide Move | Informal / Alternate | Layers Turned | Direction (viewed from face) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`Rw`** | `r` | Right + Middle slice | Clockwise (upwards) |
| **`Lw`** | `l` | Left + Middle slice | Clockwise (downwards) |
| **`Uw`** | `u` | Up + Middle slice | Clockwise (to the left) |
| **`Dw`** | `d` | Down + Middle slice | Clockwise (to the right) |
| **`Fw`** | `f` | Front + Standing slice | Clockwise |
| **`Bw`** | `b` | Back + Standing slice | Clockwise |

---

## 2. Wide Prime and Double Turns

Just like standard turns, wide moves support prime (`'`) and double (`2`) modifiers:

- **`Rw'` (Rw-Prime)**: Turns the right two layers 90 degrees counter-clockwise (pulls both downwards towards you).
- **`Rw2` (Rw-Double)**: Turns the right two layers 180 degrees.
- **`Uw'` (Uw-Prime)**: Turns the top two layers 90 degrees counter-clockwise (rotates both to the right).
- **`Uw2` (Uw-Double)**: Turns the top two layers 180 degrees.

> [!TIP]
> **Mechanics Note**: Turning `Rw` on a 3x3 produces the exact same physical piece permutation as holding the left face stationary and turning `L'` followed by an `x` whole-cube rotation (`Rw = L' x`).

## 4. Wide Moves in Speedcubing Algorithms

Wide moves are widely used in speedcubing for two key reasons:

1. **Ergonomic Finger Tricks**: It is often faster and smoother to turn two layers with your fingers than to do a single turn and a separate whole-cube rotation.
2. **Orienting the Last Layer (OLL)**: Many essential last-layer algorithms begin with wide moves to orient edges and corners simultaneously.

A premier example is the **Fat Sune** (a wide variation of the Sune algorithm used in CFOP):

```notation
Rw U R' U' Rw' F R F'
```

## 5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Confusing Slice Moves with Wide Moves**: A slice move (`M`) turns *only* the inner middle layer while keeping both outer layers still. A wide move (`Rw`) turns the outer face *plus* the middle layer together.
- **Grabbing Too Many Layers on 3x3**: A wide move on a 3x3 turns exactly 2 layers. Turning all 3 layers is a whole-cube rotation (`x`), not a wide move.
- **Forgetting the Inverse on Wide Prime Moves**: When executing `Rw'`, remember to turn *both* layers counter-clockwise (downwards), not just the outer face.

---

## 6. Summary & Key Takeaways

- **`Rw`, `Uw`, `Fw`, `Lw`, `Dw`, `Bw`**: Turn the outer face and the adjacent layer together (2 layers deep).
- **WCA standard is `w`**: `Rw` is the official standard; lowercase `r` is common in community guides.
- **Modifiers apply normally**: `Rw'` is 90° counter-clockwise; `Rw2` is 180°.
- **Bridge to big cubes**: Wide moves are a fundamental building block for larger puzzles like the 4x4 and 5x5.
