The classic 3x3 is the world's most famous puzzle, but it is only one member of a massive family known as **twisty puzzles**.

Today, competitive speedcubing includes dozens of different puzzle shapes, sizes, and mechanisms. Understanding these categories will help you navigate the cubing world and see how 3x3 fundamentals apply everywhere.

> [!NOTE]
> While the term *"Rubik's Cube"* refers specifically to the 3x3 puzzle invented by Ernő Rubik, the community uses **twisty puzzles** or **speedcubes** to describe the entire family of rotational puzzles.

---

## 1. The NxN Cube Family

The most common puzzles share the traditional cube shape but vary in the number of layers on each side:

| Puzzle | Dimensions | Piece Structure | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2x2** (Pocket Cube) | 2x2x2 | 8 Corners only | No visible centers or edges; fast and explosive. |
| **3x3** (Classic Cube) | 3x3x3 | 6 Centers, 12 Edges, 8 Corners | The universal benchmark for all speedcubing. |
| **4x4** (Rubik's Revenge) | 4x4x4 | 24 Centers, 24 Edges, 8 Corners | No fixed centers; introduces parity cases. |
| **5x5** (Professor's Cube) | 5x5x5 | 54 Centers, 36 Edges, 8 Corners | Fixed centers return; deep reduction puzzle. |

### Odd vs. Even Layered Cubes

There is a fundamental mechanical difference between odd and even puzzles:

- **Odd Cubes (3x3, 5x5):** Have fixed center pieces attached directly to the internal core. These centers tell you which color belongs on each face.
- **Even Cubes (2x2, 4x4):** Have no fixed centers. You must know the standard color scheme by heart so you do not build the faces in the wrong order.

## 2. Beyond the Cube: Alternative Geometries

Twisty puzzles come in many geometric shapes beyond the classic six-sided cube. The World Cube Association (WCA) recognizes several non-cubic events:

### Pyraminx (Tetrahedron)
A four-sided pyramid with triangular faces. It turns around its vertices rather than its faces, making it one of the most intuitive and fastest-solving puzzles in the community.

### Megaminx (Dodecahedron)
A 12-sided puzzle where each face is a pentagon. Despite looking intimidating with 50 moving pieces, it is solved using direct extensions of standard 3x3 techniques.

### Skewb (Corner-Turning Cube)
A cube that turns diagonally along its corners instead of flat across its faces. Every turn slices through the center of the puzzle, moving half the entire puzzle at once.

### Square-1 (Shape-Shifter)
A puzzle with kite and triangular pieces that changes its physical shape as you turn it. It uses an entirely different notation and solving logic from standard cubes.

## 3. Why Master the 3x3 First?

If you want to solve 4x4, 5x5, or Megaminx, your first step is always the 3x3.

Almost all larger puzzles are solved using a method called **Reduction**:

1. Group the scattered center pieces into solid color blocks.
2. Pair up matching edge pieces into single composite edges.
3. Once the centers and edges are built, **solve the puzzle exactly like a regular 3x3**.

Every algorithm, piece-tracking skill, and fingertrick you learn on a 3x3 will directly transfer to bigger and more complex puzzles.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **The NxN Family**: Ranges from 2x2 up to 7x7 in official competitions (and even larger in casual cubing).
- **Even cubes have floating centers**: You must memorize color arrangements when solving 2x2 or 4x4.
- **Non-cubic puzzles**: Pyraminx, Megaminx, and Skewb offer fresh geometries based on shared mechanical principles.
- **3x3 is the master key**: Mastering the 3x3 provides the foundation to solve nearly every other twisty puzzle.
