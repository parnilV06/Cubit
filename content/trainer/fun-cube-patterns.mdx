Before diving into solving methods and speedcubing drills, there is a playful side to the Rubik's Cube: **cube patterns**.

A pattern is created by starting with a solved cube and executing a specific sequence of moves. Because every move on a Rubik's Cube is reversible, you can transform your cube into eye-catching geometric art and return it back to solved whenever you like.

> [!NOTE]
> All patterns in this lesson start from a **fully solved cube**. If your cube is currently scrambled, complete the solving modules first or use the interactive players below to experiment.

---

## 1. Patterns vs. Solving Algorithms

In speedcubing, a sequence of turns is called an **algorithm**.

- **Solving algorithms** take scattered pieces and assemble them into their correct layers.
- **Pattern algorithms** rearrange a solved cube into symmetrical designs.

Learning patterns is one of the best ways to get comfortable turning the faces and seeing how moves interact across the six faces.

---

## 2. The Classic Checkerboard

The **Checkerboard** is the most iconic cube pattern. It alternates opposing colors across every face, swapping edge pieces across the X, Y, and Z axes.

### Algorithm:
```notation
R2 L2 U2 D2 F2 B2
```

Use the interactive player below to watch the double turns transform the solved cube:

/>

### How to Restore:
Because every move in this sequence is a 180° double turn, the algorithm is its own opposite. Simply execute `R2 L2 U2 D2 F2 B2` a second time to return the cube to solved.

## 3. Six Spots (Center Swap)

The **Six Spots** pattern swaps the center piece on each face with the center of an adjacent face, creating a clean single-dot contrast on all six sides.

### Algorithm:
```notation
U D' R L' F B' U D'
```

<AlgorithmPlayer
  algorithm="U D' R L' F B' U D'"
  title="Six Spots (Center Swap)"
  description="Swaps all six center pieces while preserving edge and corner positions"
/>

### How to Restore:
Execute the reverse sequence:
```notation
D U' B F' L R' D U'
```

## 4. Cube in a Cube

The **Cube in a Cube** pattern creates the optical illusion of a miniature 2x2 cube nested inside the corner of a 3x3.

### Algorithm:
```notation
F L F U' R U F2 L2 U' L' B D' B' L2 U
```

<AlgorithmPlayer
  algorithm="F L F U' R U F2 L2 U' L' B D' B' L2 U"
  title="Cube in a Cube"
  description="Rotates three edges and two corners to frame a 2x2 cube within the 3x3"
/>

### How to Restore:
Execute the inverse sequence:
```notation
U' L2 B D B' L U L2 F2 U' R' U F' L' F'
```

## 5. The Anaconda (Snake)

The **Anaconda** pattern weaves a continuous trail of contrasting colors across the faces of the cube like a winding serpent.

### Algorithm:
```notation
L U B' U' R L' B R' F B' D R D' F'
```

<AlgorithmPlayer
  algorithm="L U B' U' R L' B R' F B' D R D' F'"
  title="The Anaconda (Snake)"
  description="Twists edge colors across adjacent faces in a continuous trail"
/>

### How to Restore:
Execute the inverse sequence:
```notation
F D R' D' B F' R B' L R' U B U' L'
```

---

## 6. Key Takeaways

- **Algorithms are precise recipes**: Every letter corresponds to a specific 90° or 180° turn.
- **Every pattern is reversible**: You can always return to a solved state by executing the reverse moves.
- **Start from solved**: Patterns only work when starting from a fully solved cube.
- **Next stop: Notation**: In the next module, you will learn the complete WCA notation system so you can read and execute any algorithm with ease.
